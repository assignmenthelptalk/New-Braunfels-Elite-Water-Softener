## Why River Chase Residents Need a Water Softener

River Chase water is hard because NBU water is hard. In February 2023 NBU issued, then lifted, a boil-water notice for its Riverchase Pressure Zone, which ties the neighborhood to NBU's system. NBU's 2018 water quality report put average hardness at 15.1 grains per gallon, about 258 mg/L, with a range of 8.1 to 19.5, and the Water Quality Association calls anything over 10.5 gpg very hard. <mark class="data-gap">[VERIFY: that your River Chase address is inside NBU's service area, and NBU's current average hardness; its 2019 to 2025 reports do not list hardness]</mark>

A 2023 peer-reviewed study in the journal Groundwater (Simbo) reports calcium and magnesium for ten samples of NBU system water taken in 2020 and 2021, and those values calculate to 13.2 to 15.9 grains per gallon, which agrees with the 2018 figure. At 15 gpg, a household of four using 320 gallons a day removes about 4,800 grains daily, so a 48,000-grain salt-based softener regenerates about every 10 days.

## About River Chase Water Quality

NBU treats most of its water at the Gruene Road Surface Water Treatment Plant from the Guadalupe River and adds Edwards Aquifer and Trinity Aquifer groundwater. Its 2025 report lists a total chlorine residual that averaged 2.58 ppm against a limit of 4 ppm, a Superior rating from the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality and no violations. NBU Customer Service is at 830.629.8400.

Homes on private wells are not covered by NBU's report. A well owner tests for bacteria every year and for hardness, nitrate and other substances as local conditions call for.
