## Why Seguin Residents Need a Water Softener

Seguin's water comes from a river and a deep aquifer, and the city's report names both. The City of Seguin 2025 water report lists two surface water plants on the Guadalupe River and a well field supplied by the Schertz-Seguin Local Government Corporation, which pumps groundwater from the Carrizo Aquifer in Gonzales County. <mark class="data-gap">[NEEDS DATA: Seguin's average hardness; the 2025 report lists no hardness figure, so confirm with the city at 830-379-3212]</mark>

Hardness shows up at home whatever the source. Scale narrows pipes, coats water heater elements and leaves spots on glass. If a test at your tap reads above 180 mg/L, which the U.S. Geological Survey rates as very hard, a 48,000-grain salt-based softener suits a household of four. A free test gives the exact reading for your address.

## About Seguin Water Quality

The City of Seguin runs public water system TX0940002, and its 2025 report lists free chlorine as the disinfectant, with a 2025 average of 1.40 ppm against a limit of 4.0 ppm. Springs Hill Special Utility District (TX0940022) serves other homes in the Seguin area, so check your water bill to see which utility supplies your address.

Homes on private wells are not covered by any utility report. A well owner tests for bacteria every year and for hardness, nitrate and other substances as local conditions call for.
